# Ryoko Contributor License Agreement (CLA) — draft

> **DRAFT ONLY — NOT AN AGREEMENT, NOT CURRENTLY IN EFFECT, AND NOT FOR SIGNATURE.**
>
> This template is a discussion draft. It must be reviewed and finalized by qualified counsel, adapted to the actual rights-holding legal entity, and formally adopted before contributors are asked to accept it. No contribution is subject to this draft today.

## 1. Parties and contribution

This proposed agreement would be between the individual or legal entity making a contribution ("Contributor") and the Ryoko project rights holder identified in the final adopted version ("Project Recipient").

A "Contribution" means any source code, documentation, design, data, or other material intentionally submitted for inclusion in Ryoko, excluding material clearly marked in writing as not intended for inclusion.

## 2. Copyright license grant

Subject to the final agreement, Contributor would grant Project Recipient a perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive, no-charge, royalty-free, irrevocable copyright license to reproduce, prepare derivative works of, publicly display, publicly perform, distribute, make available, and sublicense the Contribution.

The grant would expressly permit Project Recipient to distribute and sublicense the Contribution:
- Under the project's applicable open-source license, including AGPL-3.0 for releases designated as such; and
- Under separate commercial or proprietary license terms, including commercial agreements with customers.

This grant would not transfer ownership of Contributor's copyright. Contributor would retain copyright in the Contribution and remain free to use it, subject to third-party rights and any obligations arising from other agreements.

## 3. Patent license grant

Subject to the final agreement, Contributor would grant Project Recipient and recipients of software distributed by Project Recipient a perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive, no-charge, royalty-free, irrevocable (except as expressly stated in the final agreement) patent license to make, have made, use, offer to sell, sell, import, and otherwise transfer the Contribution, but only for patent claims licensable by Contributor that are necessarily infringed by the Contribution alone or by its combination with the project to which it was submitted.

The final clause must be reconciled with the patent provisions of the applicable open-source license.

## 4. Contributor representations

Contributor would represent that:
- Contributor is legally entitled to make the grants in this agreement.
- Each Contribution is Contributor's original creation or Contributor has identified and obtained all permissions necessary to submit it under these terms.
- To Contributor's knowledge, the Contribution does not knowingly violate another party's copyright, patent, trade secret, or other rights.
- If an employer, client, school, or other party may own or control the Contribution, Contributor has obtained written authorization before submitting it.
- Third-party material, generated material, and material subject to additional terms will be clearly identified with its source and applicable license.

## 5. No obligation; no warranty

Contributor would not be required to provide Contributions. Unless separately agreed in writing, Contributions would be provided without warranties or support obligations, to the extent permitted by applicable law.

## 6. No trademark grant

This agreement would not grant rights to use the Ryoko name, logos, or other project trademarks except as permitted by applicable law or separate written permission.

## 7. Records and acceptance

The final agreement must specify:
- The Project Recipient's full legal name and contact details.
- Governing law, venue, notices, amendment, and termination provisions.
- The exact electronic acceptance method and recordkeeping procedure.
- Whether the agreement applies prospectively only and how previously submitted contributions are handled.

## 8. Signature / electronic acceptance

**No signature or acceptance should be collected using this draft.** The final adopted agreement should include a legally reviewed signature or electronic acceptance mechanism and a way to retain the accepted version and date.
